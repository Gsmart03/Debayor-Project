# BUSINESS PERFORMANCE REPORT
## Furniture Sales Performance Analysis
**Reporting Period:** 2014–2017
**Dataset:** Furniture Sales
**Prepared for:** Management / Business Decision-Making
**Analysis Tools:** Excel
**Report Status:** Business Analysis Report — Dashboard to be Added

### 1. Report Objective

The objective of this report is to evaluate the company's furniture sales performance by analyzing revenue, profitability, products, regions, customer segments, discounts, and sales trends. The analysis is designed to identify major business strengths, profitability challenges, potential risks, and opportunities for improving overall financial performance.

---

## 2. Executive Summary

The business generated approximately **$742,000 in sales** from **2,121 sales transactions**, **1,764 orders**, and **707 customers**. Although the business achieved positive overall profit of approximately **$18,451**, its overall profit margin was only **2.5%**, indicating that a significant portion of revenue is being absorbed by costs and discounts.

Sales performance improved consistently from 2014 to 2017. Revenue increased from approximately **$157,193 in 2014 to $215,387 in 2017**. However, profitability did not grow at the same pace. Profit declined by approximately **56.6% in 2017 compared with 2016**, despite an 8.3% increase in sales.

The analysis also reveals a major profitability problem with the **Tables** and **Bookcases** sub-categories. Tables generated approximately **$206,966 in sales but recorded a loss of $17,725**, resulting in a negative margin of approximately **-8.6%**.

Discounting appears to be one of the most important factors affecting profitability. Transactions with discounts above 25% generated losses, while transactions with discounts below 25% remained profitable overall.

The **West region** produced the highest sales and profit, while the **Central region** recorded a loss despite generating substantial sales. This indicates that increasing sales alone is not sufficient; the business needs stronger control over pricing, discounts, and product profitability.

# 7. Discount Analysis

The relationship between discounting and profitability is one of the strongest findings in the analysis.

| Discount Level |       Sales |          Profit |
| -------------- | ----------: | --------------: |
| 0–5%           | $256,025.27 |      $58,133.08 |
| 5–15%          |  $74,192.77 |       $8,530.00 |
| 15–25%         | $216,631.02 |       $6,265.95 |
| 25–35%         | $113,963.81 | **-$13,086.45** |
| 35%+           |  $72,082.85 | **-$31,551.71** |

### Key Finding

The data shows a clear deterioration in profitability as discounts become larger.

Transactions with discounts between **25% and 35% generated losses of approximately $13,086**, while discounts above **35% generated losses of approximately $31,552**.

This suggests that heavy discounting may be eroding the company's margins.

### Business Implication

Discounts should be managed strategically rather than applied broadly.

Management could introduce discount limits based on:

* Product profitability
* Customer segment
* Region
* Order size
* Product cost
* Historical performance

# 10. Key Business Risks

### 1. Low Overall Profit Margin

The company generated almost **$742,000 in sales but only $18,451 in profit**, resulting in a margin of approximately 2.5%.

### 2. Excessive Discounting

High discounts, particularly above 25%, are strongly associated with negative profitability.

### 3. Loss-Making Product Categories

Tables and Bookcases generated substantial revenue but produced negative profits.

### 4. Regional Profitability Imbalance

The Central region generated significant revenue while recording a loss.

### 5. Revenue Growth Without Profit Growth

Sales increased in 2017, but profit fell significantly. This indicates that revenue growth alone is not solving the company's profitability problem.

# 11. Recommendations

### Recommendation 1: Review High-Discount Transactions

Management should establish discount thresholds and require additional approval for discounts above a certain level, particularly **25% and above**.

### Recommendation 2: Reassess Tables and Bookcases

Conduct a product-level profitability review covering:

* Selling price
* Cost price
* Discount
* Supplier cost
* Shipping cost
* Customer segment
* Region

Products that consistently generate losses should be repriced, renegotiated, or reconsidered.

### Recommendation 3: Investigate the Central Region

The Central region should receive a detailed profitability review because it generated substantial sales but recorded a negative margin.

Management should identify whether high discounts or specific products are responsible.

### Recommendation 4: Increase Focus on High-Margin Products

Chairs and Furnishings demonstrate stronger profitability than Tables and Bookcases. Management can evaluate opportunities to increase sales of profitable products while maintaining appropriate pricing.

### Recommendation 5: Monitor Profitability Alongside Revenue

Future performance reports should track both:

**Revenue growth + Profit growth**

rather than revenue alone.

A sales increase should be considered successful only when it contributes positively to sustainable profitability.

### Recommendation 6: Review 2017 Fourth-Quarter Performance

Q4 2017 generated the highest quarterly sales but recorded a loss. Management should investigate the transactions behind this result, particularly:

* Discount levels
* Tables and Bookcases
* Central region sales
* Large orders
* Loss-making products

---

# 12. Conclusion

The business demonstrates strong sales activity and consistent revenue growth, but profitability remains a significant challenge.

The most important finding is the gap between **sales growth and profit growth**. The company generated more revenue in 2017 than in previous years, yet profit declined substantially.

The analysis indicates that **discounting, loss-making product categories, and regional profitability differences** are major areas requiring management attention.

The business can improve its financial performance by shifting its focus from simply increasing sales to generating **profitable sales**. Stronger discount controls, product-level profitability monitoring, regional analysis, and greater emphasis on high-margin products can help improve the company's overall financial position.

### Management Focus Areas

1. **Control excessive discounts**
2. **Review Tables and Bookcases**
3. **Investigate Central region losses**
4. **Protect margins while growing revenue**
5. **Expand profitable product opportunities**
6. **Monitor profit and revenue together**

Overall, the data suggests that the next stage of growth should focus on **quality of revenue rather than revenue volume alone**.
