## Executive Summary

The business generated approximately $742,000 in sales from 2,121 sales transactions, 1,764 orders, and 707 customers. Although the business achieved positive overall profit of approximately $18,451, its overall profit margin was only 2.5%, indicating that a significant portion of revenue is being absorbed by costs and discounts.

Sales performance improved consistently from 2014 to 2017. Revenue increased from approximately $157,193 in 2014 to $215,387 in 2017. However, profitability did not grow at the same pace. Profit declined by approximately 56.6% in 2017 compared with 2016, despite an 8.3% increase in sales.

The analysis also reveals a major profitability problem with the Tables and Bookcases sub-categories. Tables generated approximately $206,966 in sales but recorded a loss of $17,725, resulting in a negative margin of approximately -8.6%.

Discounting appears to be one of the most important factors affecting profitability. Transactions with discounts above 25% generated losses, while transactions with discounts below 25% remained profitable overall.

The West region produced the highest sales and profit, while the Central region recorded a loss despite generating substantial sales. This indicates that increasing sales alone is not sufficient; the business needs stronger control over pricing, discounts, and product profitability.

## Data Collection

The transactional dataset covers furniture sales records from 2014 through 2017. The data encompasses 2,121 sales transactions, 1,764 unique orders, and 707 unique customers across multiple geographic regions, product sub-categories, and customer segments.

## Findings and Insights

### Key Business Risks

The organizational financial posture faces critical exposure due to a constrained 2.5% overall profit margin, high discounting practices above 25%, structural losses within the Tables and Bookcases categories, regional net losses in the Central territory, and a decoupling of revenue growth from profit growth.

### Business Implications

Unchecked discount authorization in the Central region threatens territory sustainability. Furthermore, treating high-volume loss leaders like Tables on equal terms with high-margin items like Furnishings erodes corporate returns, requiring targeted pricing, segment repositioning, and discounting governance.

---

## Recommendations

Executive management should implement a formal governance policy requiring elevated sign-off for any customer discount exceeding 25%. In addition, leadership must launch an immediate profitability audit on Tables and Bookcases to reassess cost structures, vendor terms, pricing, and freight expense.

Operationally, the organization should re-evaluate pricing practices within the Central region, pivot sales incentives toward high-margin product lines like Chairs and Furnishings, and align corporate reporting to measure profit expansion alongside total top-line growth. Finally, management should execute a targeted post-mortem on Q4 2017 transaction data to identify and rectify the factors that caused net quarterly losses despite peak revenue performance.

---

## Conclusion

While the business exhibits strong revenue growth and healthy commercial demand, sustainable profitability requires structural intervention. The growing disconnect between revenue expansion and profit performance underscores the need to shift strategic focus from sales volume toward bottom-line profitability. Implementing discount caps, auditing loss-making sub-categories, restructuring Central region operations, and prioritizing high-margin product inventory will position the enterprise for balanced long-term financial health.
