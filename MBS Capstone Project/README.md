**Reporting Period:** 2014–2017

**Prepared for:** Management / Business Decision-Making
**Analysis Tools:** SQL, Power BI

---

## 1. Executive Summary

This report evaluates the company's furniture sales performance between **2014 and 2017**, focusing on sales revenue, profitability, product performance, regional performance, customer segments, discounts, and key business risks.

The dataset contains **2,121 sales records**, representing **1,764 orders** from **707 customers**. Total sales amounted to approximately **$742,000**, while total profit was approximately **$18,451**, producing an overall profit margin of only **2.49%**.

The analysis shows that the business generates substantial revenue but operates with a relatively narrow profit margin. The strongest contribution comes from the **West region**, while the **Central region generated a loss**. At product-category level, **Chairs and Furnishings** generated positive profits, whereas **Tables and Bookcases** reduced overall profitability.

Discounting is one of the most significant issues identified. Sales transactions with discounts above **20%** generated negative overall profitability. The discount band above 30% recorded approximately **$86,576 in sales but a loss of $33,943**.

Management should therefore focus on improving pricing and discount controls, reviewing loss-making products, addressing underperforming regions, and protecting profitable product lines.

# 4. Regional Performance

Regional analysis reveals substantial differences in business performance.

| Region  |       Sales |         Profit |     Margin |
| ------- | ----------: | -------------: | ---------: |
| West    | $252,612.74 |     $11,504.95 |      4.55% |
| East    | $208,291.20 |      $3,046.17 |      1.46% |
| Central | $163,797.16 | **-$2,871.05** | **-1.75%** |
| South   | $117,298.68 |      $6,771.21 |      5.77% |

### Key Findings

**West** generated the highest revenue and the largest absolute profit contribution.

**South** generated the highest profit margin at approximately **5.77%**, despite having the lowest sales among the four regions.

**Central** is the major regional concern. It generated approximately **$163,797 in sales but recorded a loss of $2,871**.

The Central region also recorded an average discount of approximately **29.74%**, significantly higher than the West and South regions.

### Management Implication

The Central region requires a detailed review of:

* Discount policies
* Product mix
* Pricing
* Customer/order profitability
* Sales representatives
* Loss-making transactions

The objective should be to determine whether high discounting is responsible for the region's negative profitability.

| Sub-Category |       Sales |          Profit |     Margin |
| ------------ | ----------: | --------------: | ---------: |
| Chairs       | $328,449.10 |      $26,590.17 |  **8.10%** |

| Bookcases    | $114,880.00 |  **-$3,472.56** | **-3.02%** |
| Furnishings  |  $91,705.16 |      $13,059.14 | **14.24%** |

### Chairs

Chairs generated the largest sales revenue and a strong positive profit contribution.

The category generated approximately **$26,590 in profit**, making it one of the key contributors to overall business profitability.

### Furnishings

Although Furnishings generated less revenue than Chairs, it achieved the **highest profit margin at 14.24%**.

This suggests that the company should investigate the characteristics of this product line, including pricing, discount levels, customer demand, and product costs.

### Tables

Tables represent the most significant product-category concern.

The category generated more than **$206,000 in sales but produced a loss of approximately $17,725**.

This means that generating additional Table sales under the current pricing and discount structure could potentially increase losses.

### Bookcases

Bookcases also generated a negative profit of approximately **$3,473**, indicating the need for further review of pricing, discounts, and individual product profitability.

# 6. Discount Analysis

Discounting is one of the strongest profitability signals in the dataset.

| Discount Range |       Sales |          Profit |      Margin |
| -------------- | ----------: | --------------: | ----------: |
| 0%             | $256,025.27 |      $58,133.08 |      22.71% |
| >0%–10%        |  $46,634.25 |       $7,111.01 |      15.25% |
| >10%–20%       | $244,189.54 |       $7,684.94 |       3.15% |
| >20%–30%       |  $99,470.35 | **-$10,695.32** | **-10.75%** |
| >30%–50%       |  $86,576.31 | **-$33,942.84** | **-39.21%** |

The analysis shows a clear deterioration in profitability as discounts increase.

Transactions with discounts above **20% generated negative overall profit**, while transactions with discounts above 30% recorded a loss of almost **$34,000**.

### Business Interpretation

Discounting appears to be creating significant pressure on margins.

Management should avoid treating discounts simply as a method of increasing sales volume. Every discount should be evaluated based on its impact on contribution and profitability.

# 9. Profitability Risk

A significant number of individual sales records generated negative profit.

The dataset contains **714 records with negative profit**, representing approximately **33.7% of all sales records**.

The total loss associated with these records was approximately **$60,936**.

This is an important management concern because profitable transactions are effectively being used to offset substantial losses elsewhere in the business.

The business should therefore move beyond analyzing total sales and begin monitoring **transaction-level profitability**.

# 10. Key Business Risks

### Risk 1: Excessive Discounting

High discounts are strongly associated with negative profitability.

**Potential impact:** Revenue may increase while profit declines.

### Risk 2: Loss-Making Tables

Tables generated more than $206,000 in sales but produced a loss of approximately $17,725.

**Potential impact:** Continued sales growth in this category could increase losses.

### Risk 3: Central Region Performance

The Central region recorded negative profitability despite generating substantial revenue.

**Potential impact:** Regional growth may not translate into financial value.

### Risk 4: Low Overall Profit Margin

The overall profit margin is approximately 2.49%.

**Potential impact:** The business has limited room to absorb increases in costs or additional discounting.

### Risk 5: High Number of Loss-Making Transactions

Approximately one-third of the records recorded negative profit.

**Potential impact:** Profitability may remain unstable even when sales increase.

# 11. Strategic Recommendations

## 1. Establish Discount Controls

Management should introduce discount thresholds and require additional approval for discounts above an agreed level.

Particular attention should be given to discounts exceeding **20%**, because this range was associated with negative overall profitability in the dataset.

## 2. Review the Tables Category

Conduct a detailed profitability review of the Tables category.

Management should determine whether specific products should be:

* Repriced
* Discounted less aggressively
* Renegotiated with suppliers
* Promoted differently
* Discontinued if consistently unprofitable

## 3. Investigate Central Region

The Central region should be reviewed to understand why high discount levels are not translating into profitable sales.

The analysis should be performed at:

**State → Customer → Product → Order → Discount**

level.

## 4. Protect High-Margin Products

Chairs and Furnishings demonstrated positive profitability.

Management should investigate what makes these categories more profitable and determine whether their successful pricing and product strategies can be replicated elsewhere.

## 5. Introduce Profitability-Based Sales Targets

Sales teams should not be measured only by revenue.

Performance targets should include:

* Revenue
* Profit
* Profit margin
* Average discount
* Number of profitable orders

This would encourage sales growth that contributes to the bottom line.

## 6. Monitor Product-Level Profitability

Management should create a regular product profitability report identifying:

* Top revenue products
* Top profit products
* Loss-making products
* Products with excessive discounts
* Products with declining margins

# 14. Conclusion

The Furniture Sales dataset demonstrates a business with **strong revenue generation but limited overall profitability**.

The most important finding is that sales volume alone does not guarantee financial performance. The company generated approximately **$742,000 in sales**, but only **$18,451 in profit**, resulting in a **2.49% profit margin**.

The analysis identifies **discount management, Tables, Bookcases, and Central-region performance** as major areas requiring management attention. At the same time, **Chairs, Furnishings, the West region, and the South region** demonstrate positive profitability characteristics that can provide useful benchmarks for the business.

The next stage should therefore focus on building a Power BI dashboard that allows management to monitor these indicators interactively and identify changes in sales, profit, discounts, products, customers, and regions.

**Overall management focus should shift from simply asking "How much did we sell?" to asking "How much value did we create from those sales?"**
